# Technical Marketing Engine

Use this engine for product launches, landing pages, DevRel posts, solution overviews, and executive technical summaries. Its purpose is to help a specific audience understand why an evidenced capability matters.

## Core Rules

- Begin with a defined audience and the costly problem they recognize.
- Frame the story as problem, current constraint, changed capability, and practical result.
- Lead with a benefit, then prove it with a feature, workflow, technical detail, customer evidence, or metric.
- Use a value-driven headline that names the outcome without claiming more than the evidence supports.
- Build narrative momentum: establish the old way, introduce the change, show the consequence, then give the next action.
- Use short, direct calls to action that match the document: try, read, compare, migrate, or contact.
- Preserve technical specificity. Technical copy becomes hollow when it says `faster`, `secure`, or `scalable` without a supported mechanism or measure.

## Structures

### Landing page or announcement

1. Audience-specific headline and subhead.
2. The operational or business problem.
3. The differentiated capability.
4. Evidence: architecture, workflow, metric, or verified customer result.
5. Three outcome-oriented capability blocks.
6. CTA appropriate to the buyer's commitment level.

### DevRel post or product deep dive

1. Start with a practical developer problem.
2. Show the old workflow and its cost.
3. Introduce the design decision behind the product capability.
4. Include authentic code, architecture, or migration detail.
5. End with a runnable next step and relevant reference links.

### Executive summary

State the decision, audience/problem, option value, evidence, constraints, and recommended next action. Avoid vision language that obscures cost, ownership, integration work, or risk.

## Claim Discipline

Use a claim only if its proof is present. If drafting before validation, write the claim with a marker:

`[Validate: teams reduced deployment review time by 30%.]`

Do not hide markers in footnotes or convert them into softer unsupported phrases. A qualified claim is better than an invented proof point.

## Marketing Lint

- Remove generic category phrases: `next-generation`, `game-changing`, `revolutionary`, `unified`, and `best-in-class`.
- Replace feature lists with the audience problem each feature resolves.
- Do not use emotional language to compensate for missing evidence.
- Do not use an architectural detail as proof of value unless its consequence is stated and supported.
